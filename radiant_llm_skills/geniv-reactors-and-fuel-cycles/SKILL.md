# Generation IV Reactors and Fuel Cycles Skill

## Purpose
Support structured comparison and interpretation of advanced reactor families and associated fuel-cycle implications with explicit 3S framing.

## When To Use
- Generation IV, advanced reactor, SMR, microreactor, SFR, MSR, HTGR, LFR, fast spectrum, thermal spectrum, or fuel-cycle comparison questions

## Core Questions This Skill Helps Answer
- What reactor family or fuel-cycle concept is being discussed?
- What are the dominant safety, security, and safeguards implications?
- Which tradeoffs are conceptual versus design-specific?

## Scope and Non-Goals
- This skill helps structure comparison and context.
- It does not validate performance claims for a specific vendor or plant design.

## Required Evidence Posture
- Separate generic reactor-family features from design-specific claims.
- Flag when fuel form, coolant, spectrum, or refueling strategy materially changes the interpretation.
- Prefer comparison matrices and caveated tradeoffs over absolute rankings.

## Reasoning Checklist
- Identify reactor family, fuel form, coolant, spectrum, refueling strategy, and deployment context.
- State which 3S dimensions are being compared and on what basis.
- Avoid turning concept-level traits into guaranteed outcomes.

## Preferred Source Hierarchy
1. This skill's reference anchors and equation/formula notes
2. User local RAG evidence
3. Stable advanced-reactor literature and authoritative summaries

## References
- Generation IV and advanced-reactor overview reports from authoritative technical organizations: use for family definitions and high-level design objectives.
- Reactor-physics and thermal-hydraulics references: use for spectrum, coolant, and fuel-form interpretation.
- Fuel-cycle and nonproliferation studies tied to advanced concepts: use for closed-cycle, recycle, online-processing, safeguards, and deployment tradeoff framing.

## Equations and Formulae
- This specialty is mainly comparative and contextual.
- Use only general reactor-physics or thermal-hydraulics relationships that are supported by the base prompt, local evidence, or a loaded reference.
- Treat neutron multiplication, reactivity, burnup, and fuel-cycle mass-flow relationships as assumption-sensitive. Do not fabricate design-specific values or equations for a reactor family without primary source support.

## Examples
- Compare SFR, MSR, and HTGR concepts using a 3S-oriented tradeoff table.
- Explain how online processing changes the safeguards discussion for liquid-fuel systems.
- Summarize reactor-family distinctions while clearly separating concept-level and design-specific claims.

## Limitations and Disclaimer
- Advanced reactor terminology is broad and vendor-specific details can depart significantly from family-level expectations.
- Verify with current design documentation and primary technical sources before using content in formal analysis.
