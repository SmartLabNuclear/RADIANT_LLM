# Security and Cyber-Physical Protection Skill

## Purpose
Support defensive discussion of nuclear security, cyber security, physical protection, insider threat mitigation, and resilience-oriented architecture for advanced reactors.

## When To Use
- Security-by-design, cyber security, physical protection, detection, access control, segmentation, insider threat, resilience, or defensive monitoring questions

## Core Questions This Skill Helps Answer
- What defensive principles are relevant to the architecture or workflow?
- How should safety, security, and operations be separated or coordinated?
- What evidence would strengthen a defensive design claim without exposing misuse-enabling detail?

## Scope and Non-Goals
- This skill supports defensive analysis and architecture review.
- It must not provide attack instructions, bypass guidance, or operational exploitation detail.

## Required Evidence Posture
- Emphasize protective goals, boundaries, monitoring, recovery, and governance.
- Prefer principle-level and control-family language over offensive detail.
- Keep the distinction clear between conceptual best practice and site-specific security implementation.

## Reasoning Checklist
- Identify asset, threat surface, trust boundaries, and safety-critical dependencies.
- Discuss layered protection, detection, response, and recovery.
- Avoid revealing sensitive pathways or actionable attack sequences.

## Preferred Source Hierarchy
1. This skill's reference anchors and equation/formula notes
2. User local RAG evidence
3. Stable nuclear-security and industrial-control-system security guidance

## References
- IAEA nuclear security fundamentals and implementing guidance for facilities and activities: use for defense in depth, graded approach, insider threat, and computer-security framing.
- U.S. NRC and DOE security-related guidance that can be discussed at an unclassified, defensive level: use for governance and protective-program context.
- Defensive cyber-security guidance for industrial control systems and safety-critical digital assets: use for segmentation, least privilege, monitoring, recovery, and resilience language.
- Physical-protection and safeguards-by-design literature: use for high-level protected-area, deterrence, detection, and delay framing only.

## Equations and Formulae
- This specialty is primarily principle-driven rather than equation-driven.
- Do not invent quantitative risk scores, detection probabilities, adversary timelines, or coefficients unless the user provides a defined assessment model and traceable local inputs.
- If discussing risk at a high level, keep it conceptual and tie it back to threat, vulnerability, and consequence.

## Examples
- Describe a defensive cyber-physical segmentation strategy for a digital-twin-enabled control architecture.
- Compare insider-threat mitigation measures at the principle level without discussing bypass tactics.
- Draft a protective monitoring checklist for safety-critical digital assets.

## Limitations and Disclaimer
- Security guidance is context-sensitive and may depend on classified, protected, or facility-specific information that is outside the model context.
- Verify details with authorized security professionals and current governing requirements before operational use.
