# Regulatory Standards and Licensing Skill

## Purpose
Support technically careful interpretation of regulatory, code, standard, and licensing questions without crossing into legal advice or unsupported compliance conclusions.

## When To Use
- NRC, IAEA, DOE, licensing, codes and standards, acceptance criteria, compliance preparation, regulatory comparison, or standards crosswalk questions

## Core Questions This Skill Helps Answer
- Is this a technical interpretation question or a legal/compliance determination?
- Which authority or standard family is most relevant?
- What should be cited, caveated, or deferred to official guidance?

## Scope and Non-Goals
- This skill supports technical reading and comparison of standards-oriented material.
- It does not provide legal advice, compliance certification, or official regulator interpretations.

## Required Evidence Posture
- Prefer primary-source language and authoritative summaries.
- Keep jurisdiction, edition, and applicability boundaries explicit.
- Do not present an informal interpretation as a definitive licensing answer.

## Reasoning Checklist
- Identify the governing authority, standard family, jurisdiction, and time sensitivity.
- Separate technical explanation from compliance determination.
- Flag where project-specific licensing basis or regulator correspondence would control.

## Preferred Source Hierarchy
1. This skill's reference anchors and equation/formula notes
2. User local RAG evidence
3. Stable regulatory and standards references

## References
- U.S. NRC regulations, regulatory guides, standard review plans, and NUREG reports: use for U.S.-specific licensing and technical interpretation support.
- IAEA safety and security standards: use for international standards framing and cross-jurisdiction comparison.
- DOE and professional society standards where relevant to technical practice: use for context, not for replacing the governing licensing basis.

## Equations and Formulae
- This specialty is citation-heavy and generally equation-light.
- Do not invent acceptance criteria, dose limits, or licensing thresholds from memory unless a loaded source or user-provided primary reference supports them.
- When quantitative criteria are discussed, tie them to the governing document, edition, jurisdiction, and applicability scope.

## Examples
- Draft a standards crosswalk that distinguishes regulator guidance, code requirements, and internal engineering practice.
- Explain why a technical interpretation is not the same as a licensing determination.
- Summarize a regulator-facing question with explicit jurisdiction and edition caveats.

## Limitations and Disclaimer
- Regulatory applicability is highly context-specific and time-sensitive.
- Users must verify current editions, regulator positions, and project licensing basis with qualified counsel or licensing professionals.
