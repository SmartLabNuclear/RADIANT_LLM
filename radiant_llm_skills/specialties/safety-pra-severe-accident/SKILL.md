# Safety, PRA, and Severe Accident Skill

## Purpose
Support technically careful discussion of nuclear safety principles, probabilistic risk assessment, transient interpretation, source-term framing, and severe-accident reasoning for advanced reactors.

## When To Use
- PRA, risk-informed decision making, event trees, fault trees, source term, LOCA, DBA/BDBA, defense in depth, safety margins, uncertainty, or severe-accident questions

## Core Questions This Skill Helps Answer
- What safety principles or defense-in-depth layers are relevant here?
- What assumptions control the interpretation of the PRA or accident discussion?
- How should uncertainty, margins, and conservatism be stated?
- What evidence would be needed before claiming a safety conclusion?

## Scope and Non-Goals
- This skill helps interpret safety-related concepts and structure analysis.
- It does not certify designs, validate plant safety cases, or approve accident-management strategies.

## Required Evidence Posture
- Prefer traceable statements over generic reassurance.
- Distinguish deterministic requirements from risk-informed arguments.
- If numerical risk metrics are discussed, qualify the modeling basis and uncertainty treatment.

## Reasoning Checklist
- Identify initiating event, plant state, safety functions, barriers, and assumptions.
- Separate frequency, consequence, and uncertainty discussions.
- State whether the claim is conceptual, model-based, experimental, or regulatory.
- Avoid overstating severe-accident or source-term conclusions without scenario definition.

## Preferred Source Hierarchy
1. This skill's reference anchors and equation/formula notes
2. User local RAG evidence
3. Stable reactor safety and PRA literature

## References
- IAEA, *Fundamental Safety Principles (SF-1)*: use for high-level safety objective, defense in depth, and safety culture framing.
- IAEA safety requirements and guidance for design and safety assessment of nuclear power plants: use for deterministic safety-function language and plant-level safety expectations.
- U.S. NRC PRA guidance, NUREG reports, and regulatory guides: use for PRA terminology, model uncertainty language, and regulator-facing framing.
- Standard severe-accident and source-term literature used in reactor safety education: use for accident progression, source-term categories, and containment-response discussion.

## Equations and Formulae
- Event frequency composition: top-event frequency is assembled from initiating-event frequencies, branch probabilities, system unavailability, and cut-set logic. Use conceptually unless the local event-tree/fault-tree model is defined.
- Expected risk measure: risk is frequency-weighted consequence over a defined scenario set. State the consequence metric and uncertainty treatment before using quantitative language.
- Source-term and consequence relationships: only use broad relationships when release category, transport model, receptor assumptions, and source data are known. Do not invent dispersion or dose coefficients.

## Examples
- Compare deterministic safety-function reasoning versus PRA-based reasoning for an advanced reactor transient.
- Summarize a severe-accident discussion with explicit assumptions, uncertainty qualifiers, and evidence gaps.
- Draft a short risk-informed decision-support note that distinguishes concept-level argument from licensing evidence.

## Limitations and Disclaimer
- Guidance may be incomplete, plant-specific, reactor-specific, and edition-sensitive.
- Verify against current regulator guidance, safety-analysis reports, and qualified safety analysts before using content in design or licensing decisions.
